### F1 — `owned_reads` is false under its stated hypotheses (major, fixed)

**Defect.** The drafted statement assumed only `CondV H`, `stepKind H = some .ordinary` and
`roleSets H = some rs`. Its proof needs "the owner's active root entry is not consumed". That is
a consequence of the barrier, hence of `InL H`; V alone does not give it.

**Counterexample** (T1). Take `H = [[], [(0, H₁)]]` with `H₁ = [[(1,∅)], [(2,J), (1,∅)]]` and
`J = [[(3, [[(4,∅)]])]]`.
- V, I, H and S hold, and A fails.
- The step is ordinary, with `t = 3`, `r = 1` and an owner root.
- The root key `h_1(H₁) < h_3(J)`, so the scan consumes the only root entry (cursor 1). The
  reference throws for `n ≥ 1`.
- So `K = []` and `D = [A_0, A_1]`.
- The `T`-owned pair `elem A_⟨[(1,0)],0⟩ T` and the edges of the query slot `U_⟨⟨[(1,0)],1⟩,0⟩`
  read `A_⟨[(1,0)],0⟩ ∉ D ∪ {T} ∪ Q`.

**Fix.**
- Lean: the hypothesis `hV : CondV H` becomes `hL : InL H`.
- Doc: the L6.2 statement, the counterexample, and proof step 5, which now cites the
  definedness barrier (R8 at the closing entry), U1 `cursor_nu` and R7.
- The only consumer (U5 L8.6) has `InL H`.

### F2 — the (6) induction did not carry its key invariant (major, fixed)

**Defect.** The drafted L4.3b induction carried only `b_q ∈ E` and `B ≤ E.heightAt b_q`. Its
step 2 used "`C`, the current height of `j`, caps `X_j`", and step 4 used "`C` agrees with `X_j`
before the cut". Neither follows from that invariant once an inner move has relocated the cap:
the current height of `j` is then a moved subtree, not a literal cap of `X_j`.
- `repair_output` step 2.4 ("`w.H` and `E.truncAfter bm` agree except at `bm`") rests on the
  same unproved invariant.
- So does `repair_output_exists` step 3.1 (`E < w.H`).

**Fix.**
- The invariant now includes `X_q = litCap E c_q k p` with `IsUpward E c_q k p`, where `c_q` is
  the relocated cap column. This is the paper's "the moved subtree is the same literal cap"
  (txt:288).
- It is obtained from U2 L3.1h (`applyMoves_litCap`) in the explicit address form R18 (new
  stub `applyMoves_litCap_addr`). The interval step cites U2 L3.1f (`interval_cap`) and L3.1c
  (`word_litCap`).
- L4.3b gains item 1, `w.H = litCap E c' k p` with `c' = ⟨bm.heightPath ++ ρ, c.col⟩`. The
  Lean `marked_height_mono` was strengthened to match.
- `repair_output` steps 2.2 and 2.4 and `repair_output_exists` step 3.1 now cite item 1. Step
  3.1 becomes U2 `lt_litCap`.

**Evidence.** Bounded test T4(b), 942,009 pairs: `w.H = litCap E c' k p`, upward;
`bm.heightPath <+: c'.path`; and `B ≤ B_good`, with `B_good > B` in 12,844 cases. 0 failures.

### F3 — the `litCap_spec` stub (R11) was false (major, fixed)

**Defect.** The clause `∀ e ≠ ⟨c,k⟩, K.entryAt e = E.entryAt e` fails for every container of
`c`, whose height is truncated. Example: `L_2 = [[], [(0, [[(1,∅)]])]]`, with `c` the inner
column, `k = 1` and `p = 1`. Then `litCap` changes the outer entry's height to
`[[(1,∅),(1,∅)]]` (checked).

**Fix.** Two clauses replace it:
- labels are kept for every `e ≠ ⟨c,k⟩`;
- `entryAt` is kept for every `e ≠ ⟨c,k⟩` that does not contain `c`.

The uses (L4.2d step 2, L4.3b step 3, L4.3 step 2.4) only need these.

### F4 — the `moves_violate` stub (R13) was vacuous (major, fixed)

**Defect.** The stub said "∃ X, …" with `X` unrelated to `w`, so it carried no information for
L4.3b or L4.3c. Its last clause (`m.col.container w.col = …`) also silently assumed that moves
are applied inner-first; this was the drafter's OP3.

**Fix.**
- The stub is restated in the current expression
  `X := applyMoves (litCap E w.col w.cut w.par) (w.moves.take idx)`: last entry `j` contains
  the stopping column of `X`, `i < j`, a same-parent run, and `H_i < C`. This is the content of
  U2's `MoveOK`.
- The repaired proofs of L4.3b (F2) and L4.3c (F7) are order-independent, so OP3 is resolved.

### F5 — PS1 and OP2 are refuted: the cap and child branches are reachable (major, fixed)

**Defect.** The draft claimed that no reachable scan record has a `capAt` or `childEnds` first
difference. On that basis it proposed PS1 (drop Cases 2–3 of §8.5, descendant and repair records,
and cap anchors: about 6,000 LOC) and left those branches untested (OP2).

**Counterexample** (T5, replayed). The standard
`E = [[], [(0, [[(1,∅),(0,∅)]])], [(1, [[(2,∅)]]), (1,∅)]]`, size 11, obtained from seed `L_3`
with parameters `1,2,1,0,0,2,1,1,1,0,0,1,0,2,1,0,0,1,0,0,1,1,1`.
- `t = 2`, `r = 1`, `x_0 = [[((true,0),∅), ((false,0),∅)]]` and `y_0 = [[((true,0),∅)]]`.
- At the closed state `(0,0)` the target column ends first: `capAt` at cut 1, `p = 0`.
- `E.truncAfter a` is in L, so the record is admitted.

**Cause.** The drafter's generator `gen(maxsteps, …)` stops breadth-first at depth 11 (or 8).
This expression needs 23 operations.

**Further fact (proved).** If a scan record is `wholeEnds`, every descendant record over it is
`wholeEnds` too:
- `y` is a proper column-prefix of `x`;
- a difference of `A'` from `y` inside `y`'s columns would make `A' > x`.

So the draft's descendant-based route to the branches could not have worked either. The branches
are reached directly instead.

**Fix.**
- PS1 is marked refuted, and OP2 and OP3 are marked resolved.
- The Overview, L4.2c, L4.2d, L4.3, L4.3b and L4.3c "Testable" lines and §T are corrected with
  the new runs (Tests below).

### F6–F12 — imprecise or circular citations, and two missing proof cases (minor, all fixed)

- **F6.** D4.1b item 1 (repair records) and L4.3 step 2.6 cited R12 for
  `(moveAddr ms b).col.num 0 = b.col.num 0`. R12 assumes an `AdmCap` with all moves outside
  `B`, but an admitted repair record carries only a `RepairList`. Now cited as the new R19(a)
  `moveAddr_num`: a move rewrites only entry indices, and `ColAddr.num` reads only column
  indices.
- **F7.** L4.3c step 1 cited R12 for "earlier moves carry `B` unchanged". R12's hypothesis
  `hout` (no move inside `B`) is the lemma's own conclusion, so the citation is circular.
  - Now: the moves before the first one inside `B` form a `RepairList` for `b`. This follows
    from R13 (fixed form) and U2 `RepairStepOK`.
  - The new R19(b) `repairList_height` then gives the unchanged height.
- **F8.** L4.2c step 4(c), repair records, cited R12/R13, which are facts about an `AdmCap`,
  for the same-parent block. The block comes from the record's own `RepairList` (U2
  `RepairStepOK`). Step 4(b3), cap anchors, now cites R13 only.
- **F9.** L4.2c step 4(b3) transferred the decorated `H`-path to `E` with L4.2b item 1.
  - At the vertex `t` itself, the decorated `H`-list `[y_0,…,y_{|Q|−1}, ⊤]` and the capped
    `E`-list at `b.idx < |Q|` differ by a block that need not share a parent, so item 1 does not

  - Added: R7 on both sides. The two lists share their first `b.idx+1` keys (R15), and (b1)

  - Now `cursor_raise` (L4.2b item 2) is used for scan and descendant records alike, with the

  - Added: the explicit four-step chain of equalities at `R_H` in the owner case for cap
    anchors.
- **F11.** L7.4 step 6 cited D6.1a item 6 (`truncAfter`) for the reindexed internal cap `S_A`.
  `S_A` truncates after the cut entry *and clears its height*, which item 6 does not cover.
  - Added: D6.1a item 8 `graph_truncClear`, with a proof, a Lean declaration, and V for the

  - Step 6 now names the forest `(F.truncAfter e').setHeight e' ∅`.
- **F12.** L7.6a step 8(b) used `isConfig_before`, which needs all of `Γ(E)`, on the S2 part,
  which realizes only `restrictTo (graph E) P_E`. Added D6.1a item 4b `before_of_restrict`, with
