Scope and proof architecture
We work in ZFC and assume that for every natural number n there is an n-Mahlo cardinal. The
intended conclusion is that the entire standard BTBMS order is a well-order. No well-foundedness of
the syntax is used in any induction below.
There are three different kinds of induction. The finite calculus uses induction on finite syntax, column
numbers, and finite generation witnesses. The semantic construction uses induction on the finite size
of a target height. Inside one step of the latter induction, a counterexample is minimized by a pair of
actual ordinals, not by the BTBMS order. Keeping these inductions separate is essential.
An occurrence is a node of a particular syntax tree. Different occurrences can have the same printed
column number. Copying creates new occurrences; changing an address does not change the identity
of a retained occurrence.
The proof is organized around four obligations:
   1. The fundamental operations preserve the finite conditions needed by the scans.
   2. A smaller standard expression is bounded by an approximant of any larger admissible expression.
   3. Every seed has a finite ordinal configuration.
   4. Every fundamental operation transports a configuration to one with smaller maximum.
The last section derives well-ordering from these four obligations. Bounded computational checks,
described separately at the end, are not used in the mathematical arguments.


1. Syntax, order, and the fundamental operation
1.1 Finite syntax and visible columns
A forest is a finite list of columns. A column is a finite list of entries (p, H), where p ∈ N and H is a
forest. All syntax trees are finite. The empty forest ∅ differs from the one-column forest () whose
only column is empty.
Entries are ordered first by p, then by H. Columns and forests are ordered lexicographically, with
a proper prefix smaller. This defines a computable linear order by recursion on the two finite trees
being compared. It does not assert that this linear order is well-founded.
The outer forest starts at column number 0. A height attached to column a starts at a + 1. Within
each forest successive columns have successive numbers. When a height is entered, its containing
columns remain visible; earlier columns of its own forest become visible as they are passed. On leaving
a height its private columns cease to be visible. Thus a number on the visible stack denotes a unique
occurrence.
We use the following conditions.
   • V: an entry in column c has a visible parent p < c.
   • I: every column of a nonempty height attached to a is nonempty, and its first parent is at least
     a.
   • H: the entries of every column are nonincreasing.

1.3 Standard expressions and finite words
Let L0 = (). For m > 0, Lm has an empty outer column followed by a branch of m singleton entries.
At column i the parent is i − 1; its height contains the next singleton, until the last height is empty.
Define Std to be the expressions obtainable from these seeds by finitely many defined operations. A
seed index and a finite list of parameters are a generation witness.
The compact word of a forest is defined at depth e as follows. An empty column contributes (2e, 0, 0).
The first entry (p, H) of a nonempty column contributes (2e, 1, p) followed by the word of H. Every
later entry contributes (2e + 1, 0, p) followed by its height word. There are no closing symbols. Write
w(E) for the word and µ(E) for its length.
The word is injective: the depth coordinate identifies whether the next atom enters a child, continues
a column, or starts a later column. The same observation proves that it preserves order. A child
atom has larger depth coordinate than either kind of continuation outside that child; a continuation
entry has larger depth coordinate than a new column. The empty-column and parent comparisons
are exactly the other clauses of syntax order.
A safe prefix retains an initial segment of this word and closes its containers. Since the first entry
includes its column marker, this operation cannot create an empty internal column.


2. Finite scan facts
For a finite word W in a linear order, let ν(W ) be obtained by repeatedly deleting terminal letters
strictly smaller than the next appended letter. Equal letters are retained. Thus ν(W ) is nonincreasing.
For the relative height keys K0 , . . . , Km−1 of column c, put

                               Bc (−1) = ∅,       Bc (i) = ν(K0 , . . . , Ki ).

These boundaries strictly increase: an append either extends the reduced word or replaces its first
removed letter by a larger letter.
Scan boundary lemma. The final source cursor against a reduced query W is the unique i such
that
                                Bc (i − 1) ≤ W < Bc (i),
or is m when Bc (m − 1) ≤ W . Reducing a query does not change the final source cursor.
Proof. Deleting a source letter immediately followed by a larger letter does not change whether
that prefix is exhausted against a nonincreasing query. Any query letter large enough to consume
the larger one consumes the smaller one first; letters smaller than the larger one cannot exhaust the
prefix. After reducing both nonincreasing lists, exhaustion is precisely lexicographic comparison, as
is seen by cancelling equal initial letters and examining the first unequal pair or first ending. This
proves the boundary formula. Deleting a query letter x immediately followed by a larger y does not
change the source cursor after this two-letter block: every source letter consumed using x is also
consumed using y. Repeating the deletion proves the last assertion. □

Proof. In the forward direction the barrier puts a query at or before the active entry. Raising that

through an edge at a smaller column. Those earlier entry assertions have already been uncapped. The
proof of the barrier, using those assertions and S, puts this query below the actual active boundary.
Its edge can therefore be uncapped too. This closes the induction.

We record exact trace facts, which are stronger than equality of the returned parent.
  1. A common strictly increasing map on source and query keys preserves every cursor pair.
  2. If a nonempty source is not exhausted, replacing its last key by ⊤ preserves every cursor pair.
  3. If two sources agree before entry u, and their keys at u have identical comparison signs against
     every query key, then a run ending at or before u has exactly the same trace in both sources.
For (1), the comparison sign determines the next pair of cursors. For (2), once the last source entry
is reached, every remaining query key must be strictly smaller, since otherwise that entry would
be consumed. For (3), the two runs have the same signs up to the first possible move past u; the
stipulated run never makes that move. These arguments also cover the empty query.
One way to verify the sign hypothesis in (3) is useful below. If two height words agree for more atoms
than the length of a query height, their comparisons with that query height have the same strict sign.
Their first disagreement is beyond the query’s possible decision. Owner normalization changes neither
atom positions nor word lengths.


3. Closure, literal caps, and cofinality
3.1 A profile used only for finite repairs
For an empty height set π(∅) = (−1, 0). For a nonempty height follow first-column, first-entry
heights while they are nonempty. If there are d descents and the last first column begins with m
maximal-parent entries, put π(H) = (d, m). Those m heights are empty by H. Require

                                        P:       π(J) < π(H)

for each owner return. Profiles are ordered lexicographically.
A literal cap at a column and cut k keeps the first k entries, appends (p, ∅), and removes all later
context. It is upward if it increases the next parent or appends at that column’s end.
Profile and repair lemma. P implies S. Moreover, an upward cap of a V, I, H, P expression that
satisfies V and I satisfies S. If it also has decorated A, finitely many same-parent moves turn it into
an L cap with the same final parent and unchanged internal cap.
Proof. The first entry of the first column of a height must point to its direct owner. Thus the
first-entry branch, after normalization, is a pure tower. Different profiles decide key comparison at
the first differing tower depth or terminal multiplicity; hence P implies S.
For nonempty J, the greatest pointer-valid, I-legal upward literal cap is obtained by retaining this
branch and appending one more maximal-parent empty entry to its terminal run. Call it Γ(J). No
earlier parent can be increased, since it is already maximal. A change away from this branch loses
before the indicated append. Consequently

                              π(J) < π(H)     =⇒       hc (Γ(J)) ≤ ha (H).

3.2 Structural closure
Closure theorem. The class L is closed under safe prefixes and all fundamental operations. The
operations preserve P when present. Every standard expression lies in L and satisfies P. Every defined
operation on a nonempty V, I expression strictly decreases it.
Proof: safe prefixes. We first isolate a word fact. If X < Y , a decrease of X first changes position
α, and a decrease of Y first changes position β > α, then the new X is still smaller. Also, changing
Y only after position |X| leaves X < Y unchanged. Both statements follow at the first comparison
decision and hold under a common order embedding.
In a shortened owner-return pair, the common global cut has position α in the lower height and δ + α
in its proper container, where δ > 0. The word fact preserves S. If only the upper height is shortened,
the complete lower occurrence ends before the cut, again giving the required length inequality. V, I,
and H persist directly. Capping each affected active key before truncation preserves all retained A
tests by the barrier; the established S permits uncapping afterward.
Profiles cannot increase under a decrease of height. If an upper profile changes at a safe cut, its
first-entry branch reaches the cut. All preceding forests and columns on that branch are singletons,
since otherwise the final cut would occur after the complete branch. The remaining height is a pure
tower ending in a shorter maximal-parent run. A positive-depth return loses a tower level, and a
depth-zero return has empty height. This proves P.
Proof: ordinary occurrence classes. Fix a positive ordinary step, put d = t − r, sj = r + jd, and
τj = Tr,jd . Every output occurrence belongs to exactly one of the following classes:
  1. original retained material, at phase zero;
  2. a phase-j copy of a body column r < x < t, including all its closed side heights;
  3. the query part of head sj , copied with phase j − 1;

      phase j.

Proof: all ordinary owner returns. A pure-copy upper height contains only copies of its old pairs.

4. The finite source information needed later
A target is always a marked height B in one fixed original standard expression E. It need not itself
be a standard expression in isolation. A current source is a marked height A′ in an admissible context
truncated after that height. We admit precisely the following records.

   2. A descendant record retains this anchor, source entry, and pre-entry context, but replaces its
      source height by an admissible A′ ≤ A.
   3. A repair record has source and target at the same printed host. Outside the source height,
      its context is obtained from the original target context by an explicit finite list of same-parent
      moves and removal of later continuation. Each move records the old and new address, common
      parent, retained later entry, and deleted closed blocks.

These are finite syntax records. They do not assume that any future target can be realized.
First-difference lemma. If a recorded source is strictly larger than B in relative order, a first
parent-field difference or an additional source entry at an existing target column supplies an ordinary
upward cap of E with V, I, A, S. Its closing parent is on the target’s actual complete-cut chain.
Proof. In a scan record the common relative prefix shifts internal numbers by t − r and fixes parents
below r. A target false parent between r and t cannot occur in that common prefix: it would already
be a relative difference. Thus common internal fields agree after the corresponding κ map; cap active
keys while comparing the paths. A source endpoint at least r maps directly to its shifted endpoint.
An exit below r sees the same fixed old fields. If the paths first reach r, t, the target internal-cut
barrier puts its query below the boundary of its complete query member, hence below Z. Path nesting

For a descendant record, first raise the assigned external active keys to their anchor values and restore
unread later entries. These keys increase because the current height and truncated context decreased.
Current cut barriers preserve the path until it reaches the source host or exits below it. Only there
identify the old external fields; the preceding argument then applies. No preservation of the anchor’s
entire external query cursor is asserted.
For a repair record, each capped outer move replaces a parent-p block ending in (p, ⊤) by (p, ⊤).
Every external query has the same returned parent. Common internal fields and these responses

increases an I-legal one. A positive cut has at least two entries; a zero cut has parent strictly greater
than the direct owner. Hence the cap is ordinary. Retained tests use the old cut barriers, and the
new closing test uses the transferred complete-cut path. Profile safety gives S. □

7. Joint source packets
7.1 Names, frames, and certificates
Every role has an immutable occurrence name and an instance tag. A chart injects the roles of one

and may have equal ordinal values.
For an entry e of F , let P (F, e) be the full graph restriction to roles strictly before its slot. This need
not be admissible syntax: it can end at an ordinary role before that column’s first entry.
A height’s frame consists of its physical host ρ and the ordinary and currently visible representatives
of every externally visible column below it. Unused representatives are retained, since a later cap
can read a larger parent. Include selectors for the two representatives of the outer entry’s parent. All
frame coordinates are at most the host and precede the outer owner.
For fixed target syntax B, a positive attached block consists of a new owner s, the complete internal

                                        R(s, ρ, po ),        R(s, ρ, pv ).                              (15)

9. Deriving the full well-order
Theorem. ZFC plus the existence of an n-Mahlo cardinal for each n ∈ ω proves that (Std, <) is a
well-order.
Proof. Every seed has a configuration by Section 6.2. The completed induction gives ordinary descent
for any standard input: choose n larger than the sizes of all its finitely many query heights. Section
8.4 gives special descent. Deletion restricts a configuration and removes its largest role. Consequently,
if a nonempty standard A has configuration α, every nonempty A[k] has a configuration γ such that

                                             max γ < max α.                                           (27)

                                                     31
In the ordinary case the new maximum is below the old stopping ordinary; in the special case it is
below the removed last ordinary; each is below the old maximum. Induction on a finite generation
witness therefore configures every standard expression.
Suppose an infinite descending standard sequence E0 > E1 > · · · existed. None of its terms is empty.
Take a configuration of A0 = E0 with maximum θ. All subsequent choices can be made in the set

                                      X = ω × Std × (θ + 1)<ω .

A state (i, A, α) is eligible when A is standard, A ≥ Ei , and α is its correctly indexed configuration.
From such a state, Ei+1 < A. Cofinality supplies k with A[k] ≥ Ei+1 . The output is nonempty and
standard. By (27) it has a configuration with strictly smaller maximum, giving another eligible state
in X.
Choose a well-order of this set by Choice and recursively select its least eligible successor satisfying
these requirements. Ordinary recursion on ω produces an infinite strictly descending sequence of
ordinal maxima, impossible. Finally, a nonempty subset of the linear order Std without a least
member would produce a descending sequence by the same set-sized choice recursion. Hence every
nonempty subset has a least member. This proves the full well-order, not only an initial segment. □
An explicit ordinal interpretation can now be recorded without choosing configurations canonically.
Set σ(∅) = 0 and, for nonempty standard E, define

                              σ(E) = min{max α + 1 : α realizes Γ(E)}.

The class in braces is nonempty; choose one member to bound the minimum search and use Separation.
Applying (27) to a minimizing configuration gives σ(E[k]) < σ(E), also when the output is empty.
The interpretation is the expression’s own configuration minimum; it uses no covering expression.


10. Supplementary finite checks and their limits
The accompanying Python programs independently implement the recursive operation and the finite
conditions. They are supplementary reproducibility material, not a formal verification of the proof.
   • A breadth-first run generated 50,001 distinct states from seeds of depths zero through four, using
     operation parameters zero through three and a size cutoff of 65 ordinary-plus-entry vertices. It
     processed 20,719 states and made 82,876 operation calls, including four on the empty expression,
     and checked the other 82,872 outputs for strict syntax decrease. All five admissibility conditions
     and the profile condition passed on the processed states. The 29,282 queued states were not
     included in that structural-condition count.
   • The ordinary occurrence-map graph construction was compared with the directly parsed output
     graph on 43,996 ordinary inputs at parameters zero, one and two, giving 131,988 actual graph
     comparisons. The full loop visited all 50,001 generated states at those parameters (150,003
     visits); its empty, deletion and special inputs were skipped.
   • From a deterministic sample of 2,500 generated states, the cap check considered 350,387
     candidate upward cuts. Exactly 77,604 satisfied all tested admissibility conditions (including
     the additional profile test used by the implementation). Every such cap strictly covered its
     target at parameter µ(B) + 1.
These checks establish only their finite test results. They neither prove the universal finite lemmas nor
check set-theoretic certificates, condensation, the minimal-counterexample argument, or the Mahlo
axiom. The mathematical proof above is separate from those bounded computations.
